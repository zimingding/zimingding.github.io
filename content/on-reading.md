I would like to highlight several books thats tremendously influenced the way I think and understand the world.

## How it all started

Zeng Guofan is the first serious biography I read, back in 2024. Zeng is highly regarded among Chinese history figures,  and from his biography I understand why.

The greatest benefit of reading biographies is that they allow us to stay close to the person and observe how they think, how they work, how they make decisions.

One of the most valuable lessons I learned from Zeng was **not to change course easily**. If you have a plan, stick to it until the last minute. In Zeng's case, this principle almost killed him! But the principle is good because abandon a plan quickly will make you not trustworthy or people will doubt your capability.

After Zeng, I moved on to Wang Yangming. Wang is even more higher regarded in Chinese history, and he was both exceptionally intelligent and talented. His philosophy and poetry are legendary.

Then I came cross Kazuo Inamori. Inamori is like a modern version of Wang Yangming to me. Wang's philosophy had a significant influence on modern Japan, and Inamori seemed to embody many of those ideas in his own life.

One learning that stayed with me is that we should make decisions based on whether something is **the right thing to do**,  judged by our conscience and morale principles, rather than by the material benefit it brings us.

## Benjamin Franklin

I admired Wang and Inamori greatly until I read Ben Franklin's autobiography. If you take away one thing from Ben Franklin, take the thirteen virtues. Recently I started to implement exactly what Franklin did 300 years ago. Practice one virtue at a time while keeping an eye on the others and noting when I failed any virtue during the day.

The goal is not to became a saint and eradicate all the vice. It is to live a happier life through practicing these virtues. For me, resolution, tranquility, industry are the most difficult ones to maintain. Even the first one - temperance - eat not to dullness is worth its weight in gold. Simply binding the first virtue I can lose some fat when modern world has such an abundance of food.

One big shift for me after reading Ben Franklin is the practical mindset. After seeing how he operate I start to understand what being practical mean - to utilize time and resource in a most efficient way. While in the past I probably wasted so much time and effort on things that weren't worth it.

## Singapore and Lee Kuan Yew

In early 2026, I visited Singapore and was amazed by the beauty and wealth of this tiny nation in Southeast Asia. This intrigued me to understand how is Singapore made?

The obvious person to learn from was its founding prime minister - Lee Kuan Yew. I bought his two-volume autobiography. It changed my view of the world. Lee is such a candid author, Through his eyes, I was able to see the world from different perspectives: British colonial rule, the Japanese occupation, his time at Cambridge studying laws, the struggling against communism, Singapore's close economic tie with United States, and the emergence of China.

History is made by great figures like Franklin and Lee, reading biography not only give us a window to learn their ways of working, it also put us in context of the world and time so that we have a holistic view of the world. See both trees and forest, elevate us to a higher ground.

My two great mentors are now Benjamin Franklin and Lee Kuan Yew. I also came across Charlie Munger, another smart thinker who also admired Franklin and Lee. Munger promoted multidisciplinary models, hard science such as math and physics, soft science - biology, economics, psychology. Basically a life long journey to understand the world better.

## Literature

I forgot to mention Walden and several classics like Jane Eyre, The Scarlet Letter, and Wuthering Heights.

Walden is such a jewel that I believe it has more practical value than even Shakespeare. There is just so much wisdom in Walden can be picked up and applied to everyday life.

Before I read Benjamin Franklin I really like Wang's ideas but its just not as practical or remained somewhat abstract. I just don't know how to become like Wang, lucky Franklin has shared his secret sauce.
